FOLK GAMES: TRANSFORMATION PROCESSES AND THE CONTINUITY OF TRADITIONAL FEATURES Basic doctoral student of the Research Institute for Tourism Development Abstract. This article provides a scientific analysis of the history and transformation processes of traditional folk games of the Uzbek people, which constitute an important part of their rich historical and cultural heritage. In particular, it substantiates the significance of such ancient national games as kurash, chavgon, and kupkari, not only in physical development but also in fostering moral and spiritual qualities. The article also examines the representation of folk games in historical sources, their role in society, and measures aimed at their preservation and promotion in the modern era. Special attention is given to the importance of integrating national games into the lives of young people in order to promote a healthy lifestyle. Keywords: national, games, ethnosport, chavgon, wrestling, kupkari, physical education, folk heritage, healthy lifestyle, traditions and values, teamwork, active games, cultural heritage. Among the military physical exercises held during holidays and festivals in Central Asia, the game of chavgon (ball), played by riding a horse with a special stick in hand, was especially popular. In the game of chavgon, the opponents were divided into two groups according to the color of their horses. Each rider tried to score a ball into the opponent's goal with a long stick with a curved end in his hand. Chavgon is a game of great practical importance. It greatly helps to form such qualities as perseverance, the ability to quickly perceive the situation, confidence in victory, and teamwork. The game of Chavgon was so popular and famous that not only ordinary horsemen and servants, but also military leaders and kings actively participated in this game. “The winners were given a silk tanguk as a prize.” Poets such as Alisher Navoi wrote beautiful verses and elegant ghazals about it183. In particular, Mahmud Kashgari mentioned several terms related to the game in his work. The silk tanguk given to the person who passed the ball through the drawn hole was called “tanguk,” and the line marking the boundary was called “tasal,” while the ball, which was considered the most important element of the game, was called “tabiq.”

Chavgon occupies a special place among our national games. Since the peoples of Central Asia have long lived on the basis of good neighborliness and friendly relations, we can see many similarities in their folk games and competitions. In particular, the game "chavgon" is considered one of the ancient games, and the game originated in the ancient East as a game for herders. Thinkers such as Mahmud Kashgari, Yusuf Khos Hajib, Zahiriddin Muhammad Babur wrote about this game. information is found in the saras. Later, this game was developed in European countries under the name "polo". Mahmud Kashgari in his work "Divoni Lug'atit-Turk" touched upon terms related to the game such as "tanguq", "tasal", "tabiq". In this case, the silk garment given to the one who passed the ball through the drawn hoop is "tanguq", the designated boundary line is "tasal", and "tabiq" is used for the chavgon ball47. Zahiriddin Muhammad Babur also provided information about the chavgon game in his work "Boburnoma". It is noteworthy that the chavgon game was played by the Temurid queens in the work48. This also indicates the attention paid to the physical fitness of women in the history of the Temurids. Pirimkul Kadirov's historical novel "Humayun and Akbar" also begins with a scene depicting the preparation of the Timurid princesses for the chavgon game and the process of playing it49. The sport of “kopkari” (goat) is also a unique sport that has been played by horsemen of our country for many years. Since ancient times, this sport was organized for special events: weddings, the birth of a child in the family, and the celebration of great juveniles. After the host of the wedding or ceremony announced to the people that he was giving kopkari, riders from far and near arrived on the appointed day. In this sport, the number of participants, that is, riders, was not limited, and all young men who had a heart and believed that they could “take away” the prey from the field could participate. The winner was given a special gift by the host of the wedding. Ultimately, the winner of this game, which was demanding both physical and mental strength, would be the one with the strongest strength. In a sense, this game can be compared to the game of life, “the strong win the game.” The participants who participated in the game and won were called by adding the phrase “chavandoz” (in the oasis areas, chapagon). In recent years, the concept of ethnosport has been developed on a scientific basis. Its goal is to preserve and popularize traditional sports. The theory of ethnosports was developed in 2011 by Russian scientist Alexei Kilasov, who is considered the founder of the World Ethnosport. The World Ethnosport Society was founded in 2012 in Montreal, Canada, by two organizations: the Canadian Ethnosport Society and the world's first "Russian Ethnosport and Traditional Games" Federation, founded in 2011. In 2019, at the invitation of the international non-governmental organization World Ethnosport Society, the Institute of Russian Heritage organized the publication of the journal. The journal is published in 2 languages: Russian and English. Important decisions have also been made in this direction in Uzbekistan. According to it, special attention is paid to the promotion and popularization of national sports and folk games (ethnosports) in our country, the involvement of children and adolescents in them, and the promotion of our national games50.
